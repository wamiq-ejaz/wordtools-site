When to use each case style

UPPERCASE suits headlines, acronyms and emphasis, but is hard to read at length. Title Case is the standard for headlines, book titles and article names in English style guides — this tool keeps short words like "a," "the" and "of" lowercase unless they start the sentence, matching common editorial style. Sentence case capitalizes only the first letter of each sentence, which is how most body text and UI labels should read.

Why case matters for SEO and readability

Search engines don't rank pages differently based on letter case, but readers do notice it. Titles in ALL CAPS are frequently perceived as shouting, while consistent Title Case across headings signals a polished, edited page — which keeps visitors reading longer.

Frequently asked questions

Does Title Case capitalize every word?
No. Standard Title Case style keeps short connecting words (a, an, and, the, of, in, etc.) lowercase unless they're the first or last word of the title.
Is my text uploaded anywhere when I convert it?
No. The conversion runs entirely in your browser using JavaScript — nothing is sent to a server.
